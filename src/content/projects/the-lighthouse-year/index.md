# The Lighthouse Year

A literary novel in three parts. First-person, present tense. The narrator is a woman in her late thirties who has taken a year-long caretaking residency at a lighthouse on a remote stretch of the Northern California coast. The lighthouse is decommissioned. There is no light to keep. What there is is the building itself, the weather, the road in, and a stack of logbooks left by every previous caretaker going back to 1947.

## Status

Currently drafting Part II. Part I is in second-pass revision; Part III exists as outline and a small handful of scenes that demanded to be written first.

## What the book is about

It is about what becomes legible when you remove yourself from the conditions under which most of your life takes place. It is about the logbooks, and the way an honest record of a single life accumulates into something neither the keeper nor the reader could have anticipated. It is about whether attention to a place can substitute for the more urgent attentions one has chosen to put down.

It is not, I keep having to remind myself, about loneliness. The narrator is alone for most of the book and the book is not about that.
